## Essay: Room to read

We tend to think that reading happens where the words are. Our eyes travel from one mark to the next, collecting a sentence, looking for its end. But the empty parts of a page are working too. A margin gives a thought somewhere to begin. A pause gives it somewhere to land.

Move something into that space and the sentence takes another route. The words have not changed. Their order is still the same. Yet a line that once felt settled now hesitates, turns a corner, or crosses a small clearing before it continues. You may find yourself reading it differently.

A page is full of quiet agreements. We expect the next word to be nearby. We expect a paragraph to hold together. We trust that what disappears at the edge of one line will return at the start of another. Most of the time, good typography keeps those agreements so gracefully that we never notice them.

Here, the agreement is visible. The circle does not erase anything. It asks the words to make room. Bring it closer to an edge and watch a passage narrow. Move it back and the lines open again. There is no finished arrangement to discover, only another way for the same thought to occupy the page.

Try moving slowly. Notice where a word decides it can no longer fit. Notice the little spaces that appear and disappear around the curve. The page is not a picture of text. It is a set of decisions being made again, in response to your hand.

Then try your own words. A familiar sentence can feel unfamiliar in a different space. Sometimes that is enough to make us pay attention: nothing added to the thought, nothing taken away, just a little more room to see it.

None of this is new. Scribes left margins so commentary could grow beside a sacred sentence. Compositors set type into metal trays and let a woodcut push the words aside. Mapmakers bent names along coastlines. The relationship between text and space is as old as writing itself.

What has changed is who does the arithmetic. A machine can now say, to the pixel, where a line will end — before a single glyph is drawn. That knowledge used to live in the hands and eyes of a trade; today it can be asked for directly, from the same page that is doing the drawing.

This small circle is a demonstration, but it is also a question. If space is part of meaning, who should decide the shape of the space — the author, the reader, or the machinery that guards the line?

You can place the circle wherever it fits. Let it crowd the left margin and watch the rhythm shift. Nudge it a few pixels and a whole sentence reorganizes. There is no wrong arrangement, only the one you happen to want to read right now.

When you are finished, the essay remains exactly as it was. Nothing is added and nothing is lost — only the arrangement remembers your hand for a moment.

## Essay (Mandarin version)

我们总以为，阅读发生在有字的地方。目光从一笔移到下一笔，拾起一个句子，寻找它的结尾。可空白的部分也在工作。页边给了一个念头开始的地方，一个停顿给了一个念头落脚的地方。

把什么东西移进那片空白，句子就会另寻一条路。字没有变，顺序也没有变。可一行原先安定的文字，忽然迟疑了，转了个弯，或者绕过一小块空地，再继续向前。你可能会发现自己读它的方式变了。

一页纸里满是安静的约定。我们期待下一个词就在附近，期待一个段落紧紧相连，相信从一行结尾消失的东西会在另一行的开头回来。大多数时候，好的排版把这些约定守得如此妥帖，我们从未察觉。

在这里，约定是看得见的。圆圈没有抹去任何东西，只是请文字让出一点空间。把它推向边缘，会看到一段文字渐渐变窄；把它移回来，行与行又舒展开。这里没有需要发现的最终格局，同一个念头只是换了一种占据页面的方式。

试着慢一点移动。留意文字从哪里开始放不下，留意曲线周围出现又消失的那些小小空隙。页面不是一幅文字的图画，而是一连串决定，随着你的手一遍遍重新做出。

然后试试你自己的文字。熟悉的句子换一个空间，会变得陌生。有时这就足以让我们重新留意：没有增加什么，也没有减少什么，只是有了多一点余地，把它看清。

这些并不新鲜。抄书的人留出页边，让注释能在经文旁边生长；排字工人把铅字排在字盘里，用一块木刻把文字挤到一旁；制图的人把地名弯成海岸线的弧度。文字与空间的关系，本身就和文字一样古老。

变化的是由谁来做这笔算术。如今，机器能在任何一个字形落下之前，精确到像素地说出一行将在哪里结束。这门知识曾经锁在一门手艺的手与眼之中；现在，它成了一段可以被直接调用的判断，就在正在画画的那张页面上。

这个小圆圈既是一次演示，也是一个问题。如果空间本身就是意义的一部分，那么空间的形状该由谁来定？作者、读者，还是守着那一行字的机器？

你可以把圆圈放在任何能放的地方。让它逼近左边缘，看节奏如何改变；再挪动几个像素，整个句子又换了排法。没有哪一次摆放是错的，只有你此刻恰好想读的那一种。

等你玩够了，文章仍在原地。一切没有增加，也没有失去——只是排布替你记了一会儿，你的手曾经来过。

## Layout explanation

**How the words find their way**

The circle leaves a different amount of space on each line. This app works out those spaces, and Pretext calculates how much text fits into each one. When you move the circle, the app lays the text out again using measurements it has already prepared.

The outlines show the regions available to the words. The browser still draws the result; Pretext helps calculate the line breaks without measuring every rendered line.
